No Credit History? How to Get Your First Car Loan in Canada

First-Time Buyers · 8 min read

Nobody is born with a credit score. If you've never held a loan or a credit card in Canada — because you're young, because you arrived here recently, or because you've simply always paid with cash and debit — your credit file might be close to empty. That is not the same problem as bad credit, and lenders don't treat it the same way. Here's what actually happens when a no-credit application lands on an underwriter's desk, and how to put the strongest version of yours forward.

📌 Key takeaways
  • No credit and bad credit are different files. An empty one has no negative history to explain — and that works in your favour.
  • With no score to read, lenders lean harder on verified income, job tenure, and down payment.
  • Credit history doesn't cross borders. Years of good standing abroad generally starts over at zero in Canada.
  • Most lenders will only finance a purchase through a registered dealer, which matters if you were planning a private sale.

No credit vs. bad credit — why lenders separate them

Equifax and TransUnion need a certain amount of reported activity before they can produce a score at all. Without it, a lender pulling your file doesn't see a low number — they see a note along the lines of "no hit" or "insufficient credit history." Internally, that gets routed very differently than a 540.

The distinction matters because of what an underwriter is actually trying to answer: will this person make 60 payments on time? A damaged file gives them evidence pointing the wrong way, and someone has to weigh whether that evidence still applies. An empty file gives them no evidence at all. That's uncertainty, not risk — and uncertainty can be offset with things you can document today.

Practically, this means a thin-file applicant with steady income and a reasonable down payment is often an easier approval than someone with a 560 and two recent collections. It rarely feels that way when you're the one with no score, but it's how the file reads on the other side of the desk.

There are more paths to a blank file than most people assume, and none of them are a character problem:

Two of these deserve extra attention. If you were an authorized user on a spouse's credit card rather than a joint account holder, that history may not have built anything in your own name — a detail a lot of people discover only after a separation. And if your accounts were all closed years ago, bureaus eventually stop scoring an inactive file, which is why someone in their forties can sit down with no score at all.

What lenders look at when there's no score

Take the score out of the equation and the remaining factors don't change — they just carry more weight:

  • Verified income. Not what you say you earn, what can be documented. Pay stubs and a T4 do more than a verbal figure.
  • Time earning it. Six months at a job reads differently than three weeks, even at identical pay. Time in the same industry can count when you've recently changed employers.
  • What's already committed. Rent, phone, insurance, student loans, support payments. A lender is looking for room in your monthly budget, not just a gross number.
  • Down payment or trade-in. The single most effective lever available to a thin-file applicant.
  • Banking behaviour. Ninety days of statements without NSF charges is a quiet, powerful signal when there's no bureau history to read.

What to have ready before you apply

Thin-file approvals live or die on documentation. Gathering this before you start turns a week of back-and-forth into an afternoon:

  • ✓Two recent pay stubs — ideally your most recent two, not the ones from last spring.
  • ✓An employment letter stating your role, start date, and salary or hourly rate. This is the document that most often carries a no-credit file.
  • ✓Ninety days of bank statements — showing income landing and expenses clearing.
  • ✓Valid driver's licence and proof you can insure the vehicle. In Ontario a G2 is financeable; the insurance premium behind it is what usually surprises people.
  • ✓Status documents if you're a newcomer — PR card, work permit, or study permit. Remaining validity on a permit can affect the term a lender will write.
  • ✓If self-employed: two years of Notices of Assessment, or business bank statements if you haven't filed two full years yet.

Co-signers: when they help, when they don't

First, the terminology, because dealerships use these words loosely. A co-signer guarantees the loan but generally isn't on the vehicle's ownership. A co-borrower shares the loan and the vehicle. Both appear on the other person's credit file, and both are on the hook if payments stop.

A co-signer genuinely helps when the obstacle is the missing score — someone with established history vouching for a thin file can unlock better tiers and a lower rate, not just an approval. A co-signer helps much less when the obstacle is income, because adding a person whose own debt load is already heavy can leave the combined application no stronger than it started.

Two things worth saying out loud before anyone signs: the loan will show up on your co-signer's bureau report and count against their borrowing room, and taking them off later isn't a form you file — it normally means refinancing the loan in your name alone once your own history supports it.

Ontario and Quebec specifics worth knowing

Financing is largely national, but the purchase around it isn't. A few provincial details change what your first loan actually costs:

Ontario

  • Dealers must be registered with OMVIC, and advertised prices have to be all-in — every fee except HST and licensing is required to be inside the number you see.
  • Sales tax is 13% HST, and it's financed along with the vehicle. On a $20,000 car that's roughly $2,600 added to the amount you're borrowing.
  • Buying privately? The seller must provide a Used Vehicle Information Package, and retail sales tax is calculated at ServiceOntario on the greater of what you paid or the vehicle's wholesale value.

Quebec

  • Used-vehicle dealers operate under a permit from the Office de la protection du consommateur, and the Consumer Protection Act layers a mandatory statutory warranty on used vehicles, with length tied to the vehicle's age and mileage class.
  • Combined tax runs about 14.975% (5% GST plus 9.975% QST) — a few hundred dollars more than Ontario on the same vehicle, again inside your financed amount.
  • On a private sale, QST is collected by the SAAQ when you register, based on the higher of the sale price and the vehicle's estimated value — so a low number on the bill of sale doesn't lower the tax.

One rule that applies in both provinces and catches first-time buyers off guard: most lenders will only finance a vehicle bought from a registered dealer. They need a clean lien search, a verifiable appraisal, and a business to fund. If you've found a private-sale car you love, that's usually a cash purchase, not a financed one.

Making your first loan build the file you want

The loan you're applying for is an installment account — a fixed number of payments over a fixed term — and it's one of the more effective ways to turn an empty file into a scoreable one. A few things make the difference between a loan that builds credit and one that just moves a car:

  • Confirm the lender reports to both bureaus before you sign. Not all financing arrangements report at all, and one that doesn't cannot help your score no matter how reliably you pay.
  • Set up a pre-authorized payment dated a few days after payday, not the day before. Most lenders report a payment as missed once it's 30 days past due, and the cheapest way to never test that is to never cut it close.
  • Don't stack new credit in the same month. A car loan, a phone plan, and two credit cards in the same three weeks looks like strain, even when it isn't.
  • Read the loan as a starting tier. On-time history is what earns a better rate later — see the realistic month-by-month credit timeline for what to expect and when.

Frequently asked questions

Can you get a car loan in Canada with no credit history at all?

Yes. An empty credit file is underwritten differently than a damaged one — there's no negative history to explain, so lenders shift their attention to verified income, how long you've held your job, your existing monthly obligations, and your down payment. Several lenders run programs built specifically for first-time buyers, newcomers, and students with no score.

Does credit history from another country transfer to Canada?

Generally no. Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada maintain separate files from their counterparts in other countries, so years of strong credit abroad usually don't appear on a Canadian bureau report. You start with a thin file. Some lenders will accept international bank references or a credit report from your previous country as supporting documentation, but they can't score it the same way.

How much down payment do I need for a first car loan with no credit?

There's no fixed requirement, and some no-credit approvals go through with nothing down. What a down payment does is reduce the lender's exposure, which tends to widen the pool of lenders willing to look at a file with no score and can improve the rate you're offered. Even a modest amount changes the math more than most first-time buyers expect.

Does a co-signer guarantee approval on a first car loan?

No. A co-signer strengthens an application but doesn't guarantee it — the lender still assesses the combined picture, and if the co-signer's own debt load is already high, adding them may not help. It's also worth knowing that the loan appears on the co-signer's credit file too, and removing them later normally requires refinancing the loan.

Should I build credit with a credit card first, or apply for the car loan now?

It depends on your timeline. A secured credit card reporting for six months gives the bureaus enough activity to generate a score, which can improve your terms. But if you need a vehicle to get to work now, waiting isn't free either — and the car loan itself is an installment account that builds history once it starts reporting. Many people do both: open a small account now and apply for the vehicle in parallel.
